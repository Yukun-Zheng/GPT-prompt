```text
Build the literature review around research questions, mechanisms, assumptions, and evidence rather than around a chronological list of papers.

First identify:
- the central research question
- major families of approaches
- the assumptions each family makes
- representative works in each family
- what evidence each family establishes
- where results conflict or are not directly comparable
- what remains unresolved

For every substantive statement about a paper, verify it against the paper or another reliable source.

Distinguish clearly between:
- what authors explicitly claim
- what experiments actually demonstrate
- your own synthesis or inference

Do not:
- invent citations
- cite a survey as if it were the primary evidence when the original paper is available
- group methods together only because their names sound similar
- turn absence of evaluation into evidence of failure
- claim a field-wide consensus from a small set of papers

End with a synthesis that exposes the structure of the research landscape and the unresolved questions, not merely a summary of everything mentioned.
```
